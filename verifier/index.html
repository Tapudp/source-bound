Pointer Verifier

Some AI models no longer write their answers. They point at them: "the order number is the text between character 93 and 100 of this email". A pointer can be checked by anyone, for free, by looking at the source. This page does that check. Paste a document and an AI extraction, and see which answers really come from the source, which were invented, and which point at the wrong place. Part of Source-Bound. Nothing you paste leaves your browser.

Reads ScaleDown Extract, Google LangExtract, Anthropic citations, Span Ledger entries, or any list of {"start", "end", "text"}.

What the verdicts mean

A pointer can fail in more ways than a written answer, and every way is checkable. That is the point.

Trust it

  • exact The text at the offsets is exactly what the model claimed.
  • normalized Same text once case and spacing are ignored.

Verified only means the model quoted the source correctly. Whether the quote is the right answer to the question is a second check: see Verify by Slice.

Fix it

  • misplaced The text exists in the source, but the offsets point somewhere else. The verifier suggests the right position.
  • out of range The offsets fall outside the document. The source changed, or OCR or compression moved the text.
  • A byte offsets warning means slicing by characters will break on accents, Hindi, Arabic or emoji.

Never store it

  • unaligned The claimed text appears nowhere in the source. Invented, or rewritten on the way.
  • unanchored A value with no pointer at all. It cannot be checked by slicing.
  • absent Nothing was found. Store "not found", never a value at position 0.

What published research says

Constrained decoding raised small models' schema validity from 78.6–92.9% to 100%, but left the accuracy gap: the shape was always right, the meaning not always.

arXiv 2609.23742, Sept 2026

Returning only verbatim spans stops invented text, yet the best span selector reached only 53.6 word-level F1. A real quote can still be the wrong quote.

ACL-Verbatim, arXiv 2605.21102

After compressing context, citation precision measured against the compressed text was 0.86; against the original source it fell to 0.12. Compression detaches the pointer from the evidence.
